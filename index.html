Step 1 · Understand

One treasury, three proposals, unequal token holdings.

You are one of seven ordinary holders. Together the seven ordinary holders own 40% of the tokens; one whale owns the remaining 60%. Each actor votes for one proposal.

Token-Weighted Voting (TWV)
weight = token share

A 60% holder receives voting weight 0.600. Each ordinary holder receives about 0.057.

Square-Root Voting (SRV)
weight = √(wallet token share)

With one whale wallet, the whale receives about 0.775 while each ordinary holder receives about 0.239.

Additional information for this session: under SRV, splitting the same 60% stake across multiple wallets can increase total effective voting weight. With 8 equal whale-controlled wallets, the total SRV weight becomes √(8 × 0.60) ≈ 2.191, compared with √0.60 ≈ 0.775 for one wallet. The total stake has not changed.

Interpretation: unsplit SRV raises equal-holder efficiency and non-whale representation in this simulation, while slightly lowering stake-weighted efficiency. With 8 whale-controlled wallets, those gains disappear and the displayed metrics match TWV. “Non-whale representation” here means the winning proposal maximizes the seven ordinary holders’ aggregate synthetic valuation; it is not a protected-minority measure or proof of real-world DAO capture.

Allocation analogy boundary

This is a collective allocation problem, not a literal auction.

The scarce resource is DAO treasury funding and the proposals are competing uses of that resource. Token holdings shape decision power, but this behavioral demo has no monetary bids or payments. The project’s auction/allocation comparison should therefore be interpreted as a related allocation lens rather than as an identity between voting and auctions.
